Why it matters

Buildings are the largest energy user nobody watches hour by hour.

Most buildings get a monthly bill, not a benchmark. Without knowing what a building should have used, waste stays invisible and efficiency gains stay unproven. Baseline AI gives every metered building that benchmark, automatically.

Hidden

A monthly bill shows how much a building used, not how much it should have used. Night-time waste and failing equipment disappear into the total.

Unproven

Efficiency projects promise savings, but few are measured afterwards with a fair, weather-adjusted baseline. Funders cannot tell what worked.

Unscaled

Engineering energy models need weeks of expert work per building. Cities and national programmes manage thousands of buildings.

Building owners and facility teams

Find waste in days, not at the end of the year

Each day, actual use is compared with what the weather and calendar explain. Equipment left running at night, failed HVAC schedules and stuck valves show up as red days on a chart.

Tested by injecting faults into real meters: 74% of +50% fault days and 55% of +30% fault days caught, with 3.6% of normal days flagged.

Energy service companies, banks, green funds

Prove savings with a weather-adjusted baseline

After a retrofit, the model predicts what the building would have used without it. The gap is the saving, corrected for weather and calendar, following standard measurement and verification (M&V) practice.

Over three months, a known 15% saving was measured within 10 points in 65% of 591 buildings (median error 6.7 points). Best suited to retrofits with large savings.

Utilities and grid operators

Plan tomorrow's demand, building by building

Day-ahead hourly forecasts add up to campus and feeder load. Knowing the peak in advance makes demand response possible: pre-cooling, shifting loads, and freeing room on the grid for solar and wind.

88% of buildings meet the standard hourly accuracy limits for building energy models (CV-RMSE ≤ 30%, |NMBE| ≤ 10%) with the day-ahead model.

Governments and international agencies

Track efficiency progress across whole building stocks

The same model runs on thousands of buildings from meter and weather data alone. Cities and agencies can benchmark portfolios, target the worst performers and measure policy impact with real data instead of estimates.

One model covers all 591 buildings and 14 building types; no per-building engineering model is needed.

Engineering simulation

Weeks of expert work per building

Physics-based simulation tools need geometry, materials and HVAC details for each building. Accurate, but too slow and costly for a national building stock.

Baseline AI

Minutes for a whole portfolio

Learns each building's behaviour from its own meter and local weather. Adding a building adds no engineering cost, so it scales from one campus to a country.

Method

From raw meter files to an explainable forecast.

Six steps, all in open Python code. The pipeline runs end to end with one command on a laptop in about half an hour.

  1. Collect

    An open dataset of hourly electricity meters from 591 university buildings on four campuses, plus local temperature and humidity.

    src/download.py

  2. Clean

    Remove non-positive readings, frozen meters (identical for 24+ hours) and spikes. Merge each meter with its site's weather.

    src/data.py

  3. Engineer features

    Calendar and holidays, dew point, heating and cooling degree hours, rolling temperature, each building's typical weekly profile and recent level, and past consumption.

    src/features.py

  4. Train and compare

    Two baselines, Ridge regression, Random Forest and gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM), tuned on validation months only.

    src/train.py

  5. Explain

    SHAP values show how temperature, time of day and building history move each prediction, so engineers can check the model's reasoning.

    src/explain.py

  6. Act

    Flag anomalous days, verify savings after retrofits, and publish everything in an interactive dashboard.

    src/anomalies.py

Model 1 · weather model

How much should this building use?

Knows the building, the weather and the calendar, but not recent meter readings. Its forecast is a fair benchmark of normal behaviour.

  • Detects waste and faults
  • Measures retrofit savings (M&V)
  • Benchmarks buildings against themselves

Model 2 · day-ahead model

How much will this building use tomorrow?

Adds consumption up to the previous day. More accurate, because it tracks each building's latest behaviour.

  • Plans energy purchases and demand
  • Anticipates peaks for demand response
  • Feeds grid and campus load forecasts

Strict time split

Training ends on 30 June 2017. Nothing from the test months is used for training, tuning or features.

No leakage

Building profiles are learned from the training period only. Lag features use data at least 24 hours old.

Fair examples

The platform shows buildings at the 25th, 50th and 75th percentile of error for each type, not the best cases.

Standard metrics

CV-RMSE and NMBE, the reference measures for building energy models, plus RMSLE, MAE and MAPE.

Use it

Run it on your laptop, then on your own buildings.

Free and open source. You need Python 3.10 or newer and about 4 GB of free memory. No GPU or hardware is required.

  1. Get the code

    Clone the repository and install the dependencies.

    git clone https://github.com/ouahab4/baseline-ai
cd baseline-ai
pip install -r requirements.txt

  2. Run the pipeline

    Downloads the data, trains the models, explains them and runs the validation tests.

    ./run_all.sh   # about 30 minutes on 4 CPU cores

  3. Open the dashboard

    Forecasts, anomalies, SHAP explanations and the savings calculator for every building.

    streamlit run app/dashboard.py

Bring your own meters

Research paper

The full method and results, in one paper.

Data, cleaning rules, features, models, validation protocol, every result table and the limits of the approach. Written so that another team can check and reproduce the work.

Research paper · PDF

Baseline AI: Explainable Machine Learning for Hourly Building Energy Forecasting, Waste Detection and Savings Verification

Abdelouahab KRIM

COMPILIX INNOVATIONS · SPASU BAC Students, Algeria

We present Baseline AI, an open-source system that predicts the hourly electricity use of buildings from weather, calendar and meter history, and uses those predictions to find energy waste and verify retrofit savings. On 591 real buildings and six unseen months, the day-ahead model reaches a median CV-RMSE of 13.8%, 40% below a seasonal baseline, and 88% of buildings meet standard hourly accuracy limits.
